MGRS GPS Privacy Policy
Effective date: September 6, 2026
<!-- Covers MGRS GPS 0.9.30 and later. -->MGRS GPS (“the app,” formerly GridFix) is an MGRS land-navigation and map-plotting app for Android, published by an independent developer (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information the app handles and what leaves your device. The short version: your data stays on your phone. We run no servers, require no account, and collect nothing.
Information the app does NOT collect
The app has no user accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service, and no servers of our own. We do not collect, store, transmit, sell, or share:
	•	your location or location history,
	•	your waypoints, routes, tracks, drawings, or unit plots,
	•	your name, email address, contacts, photos, or files,
	•	device identifiers or usage statistics.
Location
The app uses your device’s GPS/GNSS receiver to show your position, navigate to waypoints, record tracks, and run practice courses. All position processing happens on your device. Your location is never sent to us — we have no servers to send it to. Track recordings and marked positions are stored only in the app’s private storage on your phone, under your control.
Two features keep using your location after you lock the screen or switch apps, because they cannot work otherwise: recording a track and the pocket guide (haptic guidance towards a waypoint). While either is running, Android shows a persistent notification with a Stop button; location use ends when you stop the recording or the guide, from that notification or from inside the app. Every position stays on your device throughout.
Location permission is optional for browsing maps; navigation features require it. You can revoke it at any time in Android settings.
Network use (map data)
To display maps and terrain, the app downloads map tiles and elevation data directly from third-party public map services, currently including MapTiler (the default street, topographic and satellite basemaps), OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap, Esri (satellite imagery), the U.S. Geological Survey, and the AWS Terrain Tiles open dataset. When the app fetches a tile, that service receives a standard web request — your IP address and which map tile was requested — governed by that service’s own privacy policy. This is the same information any map website receives when you use it. Downloaded tiles and elevation data are cached on your device so previously viewed areas work offline. Maps you import yourself (MBTiles files) involve no network use at all.
If the app crashes
If the app closes unexpectedly, it saves a technical report — the error, the app version, your device model and Android version — to its private storage on your phone, and asks you the next time you open it whether you want to send it. Nothing is sent unless you tap Send, and sending it opens your own email app so you can see exactly what is going and add what you were doing at the time. If you tap Discard, the report is deleted. We run no crash-reporting service.
Separately, Google may report crashes to us through Google Play’s own diagnostics if you have turned on sharing usage and diagnostics data in your Android settings. That is a Google feature, not ours, and you control it in your device settings.
Files you create and share
Backups, GPX/KML/KMZ exports, ATAK data packages, QR codes, and PDF route cards are created on your device and go only where you choose to send them (your own storage, or apps you explicitly share them with). We never see them.
Purchases
If you subscribe to the app, the purchase is processed entirely by Google Play Billing. We receive no payment card details. Google’s handling of your payment information is governed by Google’s privacy policy.
Children
The app is a navigation and training tool and is not directed at children under 13.
Changes
If a future version of the app changes what data is handled (for example, an optional online feature), this policy will be updated here before that version ships, with a new effective date.
